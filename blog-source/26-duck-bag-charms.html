Bag charms went from a niche thing to something on every second tote in about two years. The mechanics are simple and almost everybody gets one part wrong, which is why so many of them end up under a train seat.

The attachment is the whole decision

What holds the charm on matters far more than what the charm is. Four common types, and they are not equivalent.

If you are losing charms, it is almost certainly a spring gate. They open under sideways pressure — the inside of a coat, a car seat, a crowded bus — and you find out later. Swap it for a lobster clasp and the problem disappears.

Belt and braces: thread a small split ring through the charm and use the lobster clasp. Two independent failure points is how anything valuable should be hung.

Where to hang it

Bags have obvious loops and less obvious better options.

The side D-ring is the intended point on most rucksacks and handbags. Strong, designed for load, and it sits where the charm swings free.

The zip pull is the most common choice and the worst. It puts the charm's weight on the zip slider, which is not designed for lateral load, and a heavy charm on a zip will eventually make the zip stiff. Small and light only.

The base of the handle is the tote answer. Where the strap meets the body is the strongest point on a canvas bag, and a charm there hangs against the bag rather than swinging.

Rucksack front loops — the daisy chain of webbing loops on the front — take charms beautifully and are already load-rated.

Weight, and what it does to a bag

A charm heavy enough to feel substantial is heavy enough to wear a hole in a canvas bag over a year. Constant swinging against one point of fabric abrades it.

Rough guide: under about 40g on canvas and thin nylon, under 80g on leather and heavy webbing. If a charm noticeably swings when you walk, it is on the heavy side for a soft bag.

For anything heavy, hang it on a D-ring rather than through fabric, so the load goes into hardware.

How many

One large charm, or two or three small ones clustered on the same ring. Not four in different places.

The pattern that reads as considered is the same as with pins: one focal point with supporting pieces around it, rather than an even distribution. A single duck charm on a plain tote does more than five spread across it.

If you are clustering, vary the drop lengths by a couple of centimetres so they do not sit in a straight line. Even a small difference stops it looking like a display card.

Keyrings specifically

A charm on a keyring has a different job: it makes keys findable in a bag, and it stops you leaving them somewhere. Both are served by bulk and colour, not by delicacy.

Two practical notes. First, keep the charm on the ring rather than on a separate short chain — a chain between the ring and the charm doubles the number of things that can fail. Second, if your keys go in a trouser pocket, choose something soft or rounded; a hard resin charm in a pocket is a small ongoing annoyance and it scratches a phone screen.

Looking after them

Resin and acrylic charms scratch and yellow slowly in strong sun. Nothing to be done about UV; scratches are mostly avoidable by not putting them loose in a bag with keys.

Plated metal tarnishes at the edges where the plating is thinnest, particularly with sweat and rain. Wipe with a dry cloth, never with metal polish — polish removes plating.

Plush and fabric charms get grubby faster than anything else because they hang at hip height in the weather. Spot clean with a barely damp cloth and a drop of washing-up liquid, and never put one in a washing machine, where the hardware will batter the drum.

Common questions

Where should you attach a bag charm?

A side D-ring if the bag has one, or the base of the handle on a tote. Avoid hanging anything heavy on a zip pull — it stiffens the zip over time.

Why do my keyrings keep coming off?

Almost always a spring-gate carabiner, which opens under sideways pressure. Use a lobster clasp or a split ring instead.

How heavy can a bag charm be?

Under about 40g on canvas or thin nylon; up to about 80g on leather or webbing, ideally on a D-ring rather than through fabric.

How many charms should you put on one bag?

One large, or a cluster of two or three small ones on a single ring. Spreading several across a bag looks accidental rather than deliberate.

Can you wash a plush charm?

Spot clean only. A washing machine will damage both the charm's hardware and the drum.

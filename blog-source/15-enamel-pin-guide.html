Enamel pins are the cheapest way to change something you already own, and the easiest small thing to lose. Both facts deserve about five minutes of attention.

What you are actually buying

Two manufacturing types, and the difference is visible and tactile.

Hard enamel is filled level with the metal lines and polished flat. Smooth to the touch, slightly jewel-like, more expensive to make. This is what most people mean by a "good" pin.

Soft enamel leaves the metal lines raised above the colour, so you can feel the outlines with a fingertip. Cheaper, and it takes fine detail better — a duck's eye reads more sharply in soft enamel than hard.

Neither is better. Hard enamel feels more premium; soft enamel looks more like a drawing. For illustrated designs, soft enamel is often the right choice rather than the compromise.

The backs, and why yours fell off

This is the whole game. Pins do not fall off because the pin failed; they fall off because the back was the one it came with.

Butterfly / military clutchPoorNothing you care about. This is the one that came in the packet

Locking / screw backVery goodCoats, rucksacks, anything that goes outside

Buy a bag of locking backs. They cost a few pounds for ten and they solve the problem permanently. Keep the butterfly clutches in a tin as spares you will never use.

The cheap fix, if you are not buying anything: put a small piece of rubber eraser or a plastic clutch behind the original one. Two backs is meaningfully more secure than one.

Where to put them

Pins want a fabric that will take a hole and close back up — denim, canvas, wool, a rucksack strap. They do not want anything with a visible weave that will ladder.

  • Denim jacket lapel or chest. The classic, and the most forgiving fabric there is.
  • Rucksack straps and front panels. Highly visible, and the webbing seals around the pin completely.
  • Tote bags. Canvas takes pins beautifully. Cluster them near one strap rather than centring one, which always looks slightly lonely.
  • Lanyards and hats. Fine, but use a locking back. Hat brims flex constantly.
  • Avoid: fine knitwear, silk, technical waterproofs. The first two ladder, the third loses its waterproofing at the puncture.

Arranging more than one

The rule that makes a collection look considered rather than accumulated: odd numbers, uneven spacing, one clear focal point.

Three pins in a loose triangle look better than four in a row. Put the largest or brightest slightly off-centre and let the others sit around it. Leave more empty fabric than you think you need — a jacket with six pins in one corner looks intentional; a jacket with six pins evenly spread across it looks like a noticeboard.

If the pins share a subject — a set of ducks, say — you can be denser, because the repetition does the work of the composition.

Storing the ones you are not wearing

Pins in a drawer scratch each other. The two standard solutions:

A pin banner or cork board. Displays the collection and stores it in the same move. Cheap, and it turns a box of pins into something you actually look at.

A hard case with foam. Better if you have a lot or if they travel. Standard jewellery boxes work fine.

Either way, keep them dry. Enamel is inert but the plating is not, and pins stored somewhere humid will eventually tarnish at the edges.

Cleaning

A dry microfibre cloth for fingerprints. For anything worse, a barely damp cotton bud with a drop of washing-up liquid, then dry immediately.

Never use metal polish or anything abrasive. Plating on a pin is measured in microns, and polish removes it. Once the plating is through, the pin looks worse than it did tarnished.

Common questions

How do you stop enamel pins falling off?

Replace the butterfly clutch it came with. Locking backs cost a few pounds for a bag of ten and effectively end the problem. Failing that, double up the backs you already have.

What is the difference between hard and soft enamel?

Hard enamel is polished flat and feels smooth; soft enamel leaves the metal outlines raised and holds fine detail better. Soft enamel usually suits illustrated designs; hard enamel feels more like jewellery.

Will a pin damage my jacket?

It leaves a small hole that closes up in denim, canvas and wool. It will ladder fine knitwear and silk, and it will compromise a waterproof membrane permanently. Match the pin to the fabric.

How should you display a pin collection?

A cork board or fabric pin banner on a wall. It stores them safely, stops them scratching each other, and means you actually see them.

How many pins should go on one jacket?

Fewer than you want to. Three to five, clustered off-centre, with plenty of clear fabric around them. Go denser only when the pins share a theme.

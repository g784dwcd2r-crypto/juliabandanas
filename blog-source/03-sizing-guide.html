Sizing is the one part of buying a bandana that people get wrong, and it is also the easiest part to get right. It takes thirty seconds and one piece of string.

The method, in full

  1. Find where the collar sits. Not the top of the neck, not the shoulders — the natural dip where a collar rests.
  2. Wrap a soft tape measure around it. Snug, not tight. You should be able to slide two fingers underneath.
  3. Write the number down. In centimetres. This is your neck measurement.
  4. Add 5cm. That is the room the knot or the collar sleeve needs.
  5. Pick the size whose range covers that number. If you land on a boundary, size up.

No tape measure in the house? Use a shoelace, a phone cable or a piece of ribbon. Wrap it, pinch where it meets, then hold the pinched length against a ruler or a piece of A4 paper — the long edge of A4 is 29.7cm, which is a surprisingly useful thing to know.

A rough guide, if you cannot measure right now

Use this only as a starting point. Every one of these breeds has outliers.

  • XS — 20 to 28cm. Chihuahuas, kittens, toy poodles, most cats, small puppies.
  • S — 28 to 36cm. Dachshunds, pugs, Jack Russells, Yorkies, Boston terriers.
  • M — 36 to 46cm. Cockers, beagles, border collies, French bulldogs, whippets.
  • L — 46 to 58cm. Labradors, golden retrievers, boxers, huskies, standard poodles.
  • XL — 58cm and up. German shepherds, rottweilers, great danes, mastiffs, newfoundlands.

The three mistakes I see most

Guessing from weight

A stocky 12kg frenchie and a lean 12kg whippet need completely different sizes. Weight describes the body. The bandana only cares about the neck.

Measuring over a thick winter coat

If you measure a double-coated dog in February, you have measured the coat, not the dog. Press the tape gently into the fur until you feel the neck underneath.

Buying to fit a puppy today

Puppies grow at a genuinely startling rate. A tie-on bought one size up will fit for months. A tie-on bought to fit perfectly today will fit for about three weeks.

If you are choosing between two sizes, take the larger one. A tie-on can always be tied tighter, and a slip-on that is slightly generous simply drapes a little lower. Neither of those is a problem. Too small is a problem.

How it should look once it is on

The top edge should sit where a collar sits, not up under the jaw. The point should fall somewhere over the chest, not past the front legs. Nothing should be pulling. Two fingers should slide under the tie without effort.

If it spins round to the side within a minute, it is too loose. If your pet is scratching at it or walking oddly, it is too tight — take it off and start again.

And when not to leave it on

Take the bandana off for crate time, car journeys, unsupervised play with other dogs, and overnight. It is an accessory, not a collar. It has no ID on it and it is not designed to be worn around the clock.

Cats get one extra rule: only ever on a breakaway collar that pops open under pressure. A cat can find a snag on a fence that you did not know existed.

Measure once, save it in your phone

Genuinely — put your pet's neck measurement in a note on your phone. You will need it again for collars, harnesses, jumpers and every future bandana, and you will never remember it.

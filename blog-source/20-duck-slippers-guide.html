Slippers are the gift people are most pleased to receive and least likely to buy for themselves, which makes them unusually good value as a present. They are also the item people most often order in the wrong size.

Sizing: go up, not down

This is the opposite of the advice for almost everything else, so it is worth being clear.

Novelty and plush slippers are made to be worn with socks, over a foot that spreads when it is relaxed rather than held in a shoe. A slipper that fits like a shoe is a slipper that feels tight after twenty minutes.

Most plush slippers are also sold in ranges — UK 4–6, 6–8, 8–10 — rather than single sizes. Where you sit at the top of a range, take the next range up.

The exception is anything with a firm moulded sole. Those behave like shoes and should be bought like shoes.

Soles, and where you are actually going to wear them

Soft fabric soles are the most comfortable and the least practical. Fine on carpet, slippery on tile and laminate, useless for stepping outside.

Rubber-dotted soles — the little grip dots — are the sensible middle. Genuine traction on hard floors, still soft enough to be a slipper.

Moulded rubber soles let you take the bins out, which is the real test of a slipper. Heavier, warmer, and they do not fold flat for packing.

If your home has hard floors and stairs, get grip. This is a safety point rather than a preference — plush soles on a wooden staircase are genuinely hazardous.

Warmth versus overheating

The most common complaint about plush slippers is not that they are cold. It is that feet get hot and damp in them, which is uncomfortable and eventually smells.

Two fixes. Buy an open-back or mule style if you run warm — the open heel makes a surprising difference. And take them off for a while every evening rather than wearing them for six straight hours, which lets both the slipper and the foot dry out.

Washing them

Most plush slippers can be washed, and most people ruin a pair by doing it wrong once.

  1. Check for a moulded sole. Anything with a rigid rubber sole is generally hand-wash only — machine agitation separates the sole from the upper.
  2. Machine wash cold, on a delicate cycle, in a pillowcase or wash bag. The bag is not optional; it stops them battering the drum and stops the plush matting.
  3. Liquid detergent, half the usual amount. Powder does not dissolve fully in cold water and leaves residue in the pile.
  4. Absolutely no tumble dryer. Heat melts the foam padding, shrinks the backing and permanently flattens the plush. This is how slippers die.
  5. Air dry, stuffed with a rolled towel to hold the shape, away from a radiator. Give it two days.

Between washes, a stiff clothes brush over the plush when dry lifts matted pile back up and does more for their appearance than washing does.

Smell

Bicarbonate of soda. A tablespoon in each slipper overnight, tipped out and vacuumed in the morning. It works better than any spray, costs nothing, and can be repeated weekly without harming anything.

The underlying cause is moisture, so alternating between two pairs — if you have two — solves it permanently in a way that treating the symptom does not.

When to replace them

When the sole compresses flat and stops cushioning, which usually happens well before the upper looks worn. Slippers that have gone flat offer no support at all, and for anyone standing in a kitchen for an hour a day that matters.

Realistically, a well-treated pair worn daily lasts a winter and a bit. That is not a failure; that is what daily wear does.

Common questions

What size slippers should I buy?

Your normal shoe size, or one up if you are between sizes, have wide feet, wear thick socks, or are buying as a gift.

Can you machine wash slippers?

Fabric and plush slippers, yes — cold, delicate cycle, in a wash bag, air dried. Slippers with rigid moulded soles should be hand washed, because agitation separates the sole.

Why do my slippers smell?

Trapped moisture. Bicarbonate of soda overnight deals with the smell; alternating two pairs deals with the cause.

Can you wear slippers outside?

Only with a moulded rubber sole, and only briefly. Soft and dotted soles soak through immediately and the wet then goes straight into the padding.

How do you get flattened plush fluffy again?

Brush it with a dry clothes brush or a clean, soft-bristled brush, working in one direction. It lifts the pile considerably and takes about a minute per slipper.

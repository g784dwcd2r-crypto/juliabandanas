"Bandana" covers about six genuinely different things, and the difference between them is the difference between one your pet wears every day and one that lives in a drawer.

Here is each style, honestly, including who it does not suit.

1. The classic tie-on triangle

A square of fabric folded into a triangle and knotted at the back of the neck. The one everyone pictures.

Best for: relaxed dogs, pavement walks, cafés, and anyone who wants one bandana to fit a growing puppy.

Not for: dogs who swim, wrestle, or reverse out of things. The knot works loose eventually — that is physics, not poor tying.

Why people love it: the drape. Nothing else falls quite as softly, and the two little tails at the back are half the charm.

2. The slip-on (collar-threaded)

A sewn sleeve along the top edge that your pet's existing collar threads through. No knot, no fastening of its own.

Best for: off-lead dogs, working dogs, swimmers, and anyone who has already lost one bandana to a field.

Not for: pets who do not wear a collar, or households who like changing the bandana three times a day — you have to unbuckle the collar each time.

If I could only recommend one style to a first-time buyer with an active dog, it would be this one. It simply does not come off.

3. The snap-on

Its own fastening — a popper, buckle or hook-and-loop strip — built into the bandana.

Best for: quick changes, dogs who hate having things fiddled with near their face, and people with limited hand mobility. Ten seconds on, ten seconds off.

Not for: heavy chewers, or anyone who wants the classic knotted look. And be aware that hook-and-loop and long fur are natural enemies.

4. The reversible

Two designs, one bandana, printed back to back.

Best for: people who cannot choose, gifts, and travel. Also genuinely practical: wear the pale side for photos, flip to the darker side for the muddy walk home.

Not for: anyone who wants the thinnest possible fabric — two layers is always warmer and stiffer than one. In high summer, a single layer is kinder.

5. The oversized / neckerchief

A deliberately large triangle that covers most of the chest.

Best for: big dogs, long-necked breeds, and photographs. A large bandana on a large dog reads as tailored. A small one reads as an afterthought.

Not for: small dogs, hot days, or dogs who eat from a bowl in a hurry — the point ends up in dinner.

6. The cat bandana

Its own category, because cats have their own rules. Light fabric, shallow cut, and always attached to a breakaway collar that opens under pressure.

Best for: confident indoor cats and cats who already tolerate a collar.

Not for: outdoor cats who climb, or any cat wearing a fixed collar. If your cat has never worn a collar, this is a project, not a purchase — start with the collar alone for a fortnight.

So which one?

The short version:

  • Active, off-lead, or a swimmer → slip-on.
  • Calm and mostly urban → tie-on.
  • Changed constantly → snap-on.
  • One special piece for photos → oversized or reversible.
  • A cat → slip-on, on a breakaway collar, and take it slowly.

Whatever you land on, the style is the frame. What goes on it is the picture — and that part is entirely up to you.

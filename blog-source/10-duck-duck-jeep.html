If you drive a Jeep, you have probably found a rubber duck on your door handle. If you do not, you have probably wondered why there is a duck on someone else's. It is a real thing, it has rules, and it started with one person in Ontario in 2020.

What it actually is

Duck Duck Jeep — "ducking" — is the practice of leaving a small rubber duck on another Jeep to say that you like it. That is the whole idea. No club, no fee, no organisation. Somebody sees a Jeep they think looks good, leaves a duck on it, and drives away.

It began in 2020 when a Jeep owner in Ontario left a duck on another Jeep to cheer up the driver during a bad stretch of the year. It spread through Jeep groups, then to the wider internet, and now runs to a small economy of ducks in hats.

Strictly it is a Jeep thing. In practice it has leaked to Broncos, Minis, campervans and anything else with a personality, and nobody is policing it.

The rules, such as they are

They are unwritten, consistently observed, and mostly about not damaging anyone's car.

  1. Door handle or wing mirror. Those are the two accepted places. They are visible, they are safe, and the duck cannot go anywhere.
  2. Never under a windscreen wiper. It looks like a parking ticket, it can scratch the glass or the paint, and it will fall off at 30mph.
  3. Never on the bonnet or roof. Grit on the base of a duck against paint is exactly how you leave a scratch on a stranger's car.
  4. Nothing wet, nothing sharp, nothing adhesive. No stickers on the paintwork. Ever.
  5. Add a tag if you like, but keep it light. A small paper tag with a message is traditional. A laminated essay is not.
  6. Do not duck a car that clearly is not part of it. A pristine company car with a confused owner is not the target.

The one rule everybody agrees on: if there is any chance it marks the car, do not do it. The whole gesture is a compliment, and a compliment that leaves a scratch is not one.

What to keep in the car

People who duck properly carry a small stash. A workable kit:

  • Six to ten small squeeze ducks, 6–8cm. Cheap enough to give away without thinking about it.
  • A few with hats. Cowboy hats, bucket hats, sunglasses, tiny scarves. These are the ones people photograph and post.
  • One or two special ones for a car that genuinely deserves it.
  • A bag or tin to keep them in, because loose ducks in a door pocket get grubby fast.

Getting ducked: what to do

Keep it. That is the expected response. Most people put it on their own dashboard, and a well-ducked Jeep accumulates a small crowd up there over a couple of years.

If there is a tag with a group name or a social handle, posting a photo is the polite thing. If there is not, nothing is expected of you.

Then pay it on. The convention is that receiving one puts you loosely on the hook for leaving one, at some point, when you see a car you like.

Keeping a dashboard collection under control

Two practical problems appear once you have more than about four.

They slide. A dashboard is not flat and cornering is real. A silicone non-slip mat solves it, and it is the only method that does not risk marking the dashboard.

They fade. A car interior in summer runs far hotter than the air outside, and yellow vinyl in direct sun lightens over a season. Not much to be done except a sunshade, or accepting it.

Does it work on other cars?

Jeeps started it and Jeep owners are still the core of it. But the gesture travels: campervans, older Land Rovers, Minis, anything modified, anything with an obvious owner personality. Read the car. If it looks like somebody loves it, ducking it will be taken as intended.

Where it does not travel is anything anonymous — a fleet car, a hire car, something in a supermarket car park with no discernible character. There the duck reads as litter rather than a compliment.

Common questions

Where did Duck Duck Jeep start?

With a Jeep owner in Ontario, Canada, in 2020, who left a duck on another Jeep as a small act of cheering-up. It spread through Jeep owners' groups from there.

Where should you put the duck?

On the door handle or hooked over a wing mirror. Never under a wiper, on the bonnet, on the roof, or anywhere it touches paint under its own weight.

What do you do if someone ducks your Jeep?

Keep it, usually on your dashboard. Post a photo if the tag asks you to. Then leave one on someone else's car when you see one you like.

Can you duck a car that is not a Jeep?

Technically it is a Jeep tradition, but it has spread widely and few people object. Use judgement: duck cars that clearly have an enthusiast behind them, not anonymous ones.

What size ducks are used?

Small ones, around 6–8cm. Big enough to be noticed, small enough to sit on a door handle without falling off, cheap enough to give away.

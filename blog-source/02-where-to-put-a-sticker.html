A vinyl sticker will outlive most of the things you put it on. That is the whole appeal, and also the reason it is worth thinking for ten seconds before you press one down.

The rule that decides everything

Vinyl sticks to things that are smooth, hard, clean and dry. Every good surface has all four. Every bad surface is missing at least one.

That is genuinely the whole science. Everything below is just working through the list.

Where they work brilliantly

  • Water bottles — stainless steel and hard plastic are perfect. A matte vinyl sticker survives a dishwasher on a bottle, which still faintly astonishes me.
  • Laptops — the aluminium lid is about as good as a surface gets. Keep them off the palm rest, where hand oil eventually wins.
  • Phone cases — hard plastic yes, soft silicone no. Silicone is designed so nothing sticks to it, including us.
  • Notebooks and hard folders — the glossier the cover, the better it holds.
  • Toolboxes, tins, bike frames, kettles, guitar cases — all excellent, all improved by a frog.

Where they will let you down

  • Anything textured — brick, rough render, canvas, a powder-coated water bottle with a grippy finish. Vinyl can only grip what it can touch.
  • Soft silicone — see above. It is not you.
  • Walls with matte emulsion paint — it will stick, and it will take a coin-sized piece of your paint with it when it leaves.
  • Anything that gets genuinely hot — the inside of a car windscreen in August, the side of an oven, a radiator.
  • Skin. People ask. Please do not.

If you are not sure whether a surface will work, press the sticker down without removing the backing and see whether it sits completely flat. If it lifts anywhere, the adhesive will lift there too.

Ten seconds of prep is worth it

Wipe the surface with a little rubbing alcohol, or washing-up liquid and water, and let it dry completely. You are removing grease, not dirt — most surfaces look clean and are covered in the invisible film your hands leave behind.

Skip fancy cleaning sprays. Anything that leaves a shine leaves a residue, and residue is exactly what the adhesive cannot grip.

Actually applying it

  1. Peel the backing slowly and hold the sticker by the edges. Fingers on the adhesive are the single most common way to ruin one.
  2. Line it up before it touches down. Once vinyl makes contact it does not want to be repositioned.
  3. Lay one edge down first, then press across in one slow sweep. Coming straight down from above traps bubbles.
  4. Press it firmly all over with a fingertip or the edge of a card, especially the corners.
  5. Leave it alone for 24 hours before it gets wet. The adhesive needs that time to reach full strength.

Got a bubble? Push it out to the nearest edge with your thumb. If it will not move, a pin at the very edge of the bubble lets the air out and the mark disappears.

Taking one off again

Warm it first. A hairdryer on low for thirty seconds softens the adhesive and turns a fight into a peel. Lift a corner with a fingernail and pull back on itself at a low angle rather than straight up.

Any residue left behind comes off with rubbing alcohol on a cloth. Do not attack it with a blade unless the surface is glass.

And the sensible bit

Stickers of mine are meant to be used. If you have a sheet sitting in a drawer being saved for the right moment, this is your permission: the right moment is a water bottle, and it is now.

Ducks are having a decade. They are on hats, on dashboards, on enamel pins, in memes about being perceived. This is not a coincidence and it is not entirely random, and the reasons are more interesting than "they are cute".

The rubber duck came first, and it came from plumbing

The rubber duck is a by-product of the rubber industry. Early ones, from the late 1800s, were solid rubber and did not float — they were chew toys rather than bath toys. The hollow, floating, squeaking duck arrived with the material advances of the 1940s.

The version most people picture — plain yellow, minimal features, sat flat on the water — was popularised enormously by Sesame Street in 1970, when Ernie sang "Rubber Duckie" and it became a genuine chart hit. That song is the reason the default duck in your head is yellow with an orange bill and nothing else going on.

Everything since — cowboy ducks, graduation ducks, ducks in sunglasses — is a variation on a silhouette that was fixed in the popular imagination by a puppet in 1970.

Why the shape works so well

There is a design answer to this and it is worth knowing if you make things.

A duck reads at any size. The silhouette is a round body, a round head and a wedge. Three shapes. It survives being embroidered at 3cm on a cap, printed at 2cm on a sock, and blown up to A2 on a wall. Very few subjects do all three — most either lose their detail small or look empty large.

It has a face without needing one. A dot and a bill is enough. That means the design can be extremely simple and still feel like a character, which is the hard part of illustrated merchandise.

It takes accessories. A flat crown and a wide body mean you can put a hat on a duck and it still reads as a duck. This is genuinely why there are so many duck variants: the base shape tolerates being messed with. Try putting a cowboy hat on a rabbit and see how quickly it stops being a rabbit.

It is emotionally neutral. A duck can be smug, sad, tired or triumphant with a two-pixel change in the eye. Nothing about the base form commits it to a mood.

Rubber duck debugging

The programming tradition, from The Pragmatic Programmer (1999): when stuck on a bug, explain your code line by line to a rubber duck on your desk. The act of articulating the problem out loud, to something that cannot help, reveals the answer surprisingly often.

It is a real technique with a real name and it put a rubber duck on an enormous number of desks in an entire industry. A meaningful slice of the current duck economy traces back to a programming book from 1999.

Duck Duck Jeep

In 2020, a Jeep owner in Ontario left a rubber duck on another Jeep to cheer up the driver. It spread through Jeep owners' groups and became a genuine subculture with unwritten rules, dedicated ducks, and a small industry making ducks in tiny hats.

This is probably the single biggest driver of the current wave, because it turned ducks from a thing you own into a thing you do. There is a full account in Duck Duck Jeep, explained.

The internet's ducks

Then there is the online duck, which is a slightly different animal. Ducks and geese have been the internet's preferred vehicle for a specific kind of joke — mild menace, unearned confidence, being watched by something with no discernible motive — for a decade. The untitled goose, the duck standing in a doorway, the sarcastic ones on socks.

That register is not cute exactly. It is deadpan. And deadpan is what lets an adult wear a duck without it reading as childish, which is the whole reason this works as a range for grown-ups.

Why ducks and not something else

A subject that can carry a whole season has to pass three tests: it has to read at 3cm, survive being framed on a wall, and contain more than one joke. Cats read small but are so heavily drawn already that everything looks like something else. Frogs read small and framed but have a narrower emotional range. Ducks pass all three comfortably, which is why they went first — the reasoning is set out in what a season actually means.

Common questions

When was the rubber duck invented?

Rubber ducks date to the late 1800s, but they were solid and did not float. The hollow floating version arrived in the 1940s, and the familiar plain yellow design was popularised by Sesame Street in 1970.

What is rubber duck debugging?

A programming technique from The Pragmatic Programmer (1999): explain your code aloud, line by line, to a rubber duck. Articulating the problem is often what solves it.

Why are ducks so popular right now?

A combination of Duck Duck Jeep turning them into a shared activity, the internet's long affection for deadpan waterfowl, and a shape that works on absolutely any product at absolutely any size.

Why do people put ducks on their car dashboard?

Mostly because they were given one by a stranger. See the guide to duck car ornaments.

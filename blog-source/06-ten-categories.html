The shop is organised into ten categories, which sounds like a plan. It was not a plan. Each one started as a single drawing that got out of hand, and the category came afterwards to explain the mess.

Here is where each of them actually came from.

Ducks

The first one, and still the one that sells fastest. It began as a rubber duck drawn to test a line weight, which then got a bucket hat because the composition looked empty. Ducks turn out to be the perfect shape for a sticker: round, instantly readable at any size, and improved by absolutely any hat.

Mushrooms

The only category that came from a colour rather than a creature. I wanted an excuse to use soft pink against cream, and a spotted toadstool is essentially that colour combination with a face on it. Now there are eleven of them and one is asleep.

Frogs

Entirely somebody else's fault. Three separate people messaged in the same week asking for frogs, which felt less like a coincidence and more like an instruction. The wide-eyed, slightly startled expression came first and the rest of the frog was built around it.

Snails

Drawn on a genuinely bad week, when the idea of carrying your whole house on your back and moving very slowly had considerable appeal. People seem to feel the same way about them, which is either lovely or concerning.

Bees

The hardest of the ten to get right. Bees are structurally awkward — stripes on a curved body, wings that go transparent, and a very fine line between "cute" and "wasp". It took four attempts before one looked friendly, and the fix was making the eyes bigger and the stripes fewer.

Cats

Obvious, inevitable, and much harder than it looks. Every cat drawing risks looking like every other cat drawing, so these ones are all closed-eyed and faintly smug rather than wide-eyed and cute. That felt more honest.

Bears

Started as one small bear on a birthday card for a friend, and it turned out that a round face with round ears is the most forgiving shape in the entire shop. It works at 2cm and it works at A3, which very few designs manage.

Strawberries

The fruit category exists because I needed something in the range that was not an animal, and strawberries carry the pink beautifully. The seeds being yellow rather than the usual yellow-green is deliberate, and it is the detail people notice most often.

Moons & stars

The quiet corner. Everything else in the shop is grinning at you, so this one is asleep. It is the category that sells most as a gift, and the one that ends up on the largest number of bedroom walls.

Tiny snacks

The newest and the silliest: a slice of toast with a pat of butter, an egg with a very serious expression, a single pea. It exists because somebody bought a mushroom sticker and asked, entirely reasonably, whether there was any breakfast.

What happens next

Most likely an eleventh category, started by exactly the same route: one drawing, made for no reason, that refuses to be a one-off.

If there is a creature you want to see, tell me. Frogs are on this list because three people asked, and I have never once regretted listening.

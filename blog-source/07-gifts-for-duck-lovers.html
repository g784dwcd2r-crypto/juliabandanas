Buying for someone who likes ducks is one of the easier gifting problems, and people still manage to get it wrong. The usual failure is volume: five duck things at once, none of them used, all of them living in a drawer by March.

One good duck thing beats five average ones. Here is how to pick it, sorted by what you actually want to spend.

First, work out which kind of duck person they are

There are broadly three, and they want different objects.

  • The quiet one. Likes ducks, does not want to announce it. Buy something they use daily where the duck is small: a mug, a pin, a keyring.
  • The committed one. Owns the socks, has named the ducks in the local park. Buy something wearable and loud — a bucket hat, a graphic tee, the socks.
  • The collector. Wants the set, not the item. Buy stickers, pins or a small print — things that sit alongside what they already have rather than competing with it.

If you genuinely do not know, buy the mug. Nobody has ever had too many mugs they actually like, which is a different sentence from "nobody has ever had too many mugs".

The rule under a tenner is consumable or wearable. Ornaments in this bracket become clutter; things you wear or stick to something do not.

£10 to £20

This is the range that does most of the work, and it is where mugs, hats and car ornaments live.

A mug is the safest gift in the entire shop. It is used every morning, it does not need sizing, and if they already have a favourite mug, yours becomes the weekend one. Choose the design for them, not for you — see choosing a duck mug if you want to think about capacity properly.

A bucket hat is the best gift here if you know they wear hats. If you do not know, it is one of the worst, because an unworn hat is very obviously unworn. Sizes matter less than people expect — most are a 56–58cm one size — and the fitting guide takes two minutes.

A car ornament is the sleeper hit of this bracket. It costs less than a round of drinks, it lives on a dashboard where it gets seen forty times a week, and it is the only gift on this list that other people will comment on. Start with the dashboard duck guide.

£20 to £40

Now you are buying something with a bit of ceremony to it.

  • A framed print. The most adult duck gift there is. A small print above a desk or a kettle reads as decor rather than merch, which is exactly the trick you want for someone in their thirties. Prints and posters.
  • A shirt. Riskier, because it has to fit and it has to be their kind of shirt. Buy this only for someone whose wardrobe you have actually looked at.
  • Slippers. Enormously well received and almost never bought for oneself, which is the definition of a good gift. Check the sizing note in the slippers guide — they run generously.
  • A small bundle. A mug plus a sticker sheet plus a pin, wrapped together, beats a single £35 item for most recipients. It looks considered rather than expensive.

The gifts that reliably fail

Said with affection, having watched all of these happen.

Novelty items with one joke. They get a laugh on the day and are never touched again. If the entire object is the punchline, it has a shelf life of about four minutes.

Anything sized, bought for someone whose size you are guessing. Hats are forgiving. Shirts and slippers are not. If you are guessing, buy the unsized thing.

Five small things instead of one good thing. Covered above, and worth repeating, because it is the most common mistake by a distance.

A duck thing for someone who likes one specific duck. People who love mallards do not necessarily love rubber ducks, and vice versa. They are genuinely different aesthetics — the spotter's guide covers the difference if you need to check.

Common questions

What is the best gift for someone who already has duck everything?

Stickers or pins. Collectors buy their own big items but rarely buy themselves the small ones, and a pin they do not already own is more welcome than a duplicate of something expensive.

What should I buy if I do not know their size?

A mug, a print, a car ornament, stickers, pins or a keyring. All unsized, all safe. Avoid shirts, slippers and fitted caps.

Is a duck gift appropriate for an adult?

Yes, if you buy the restrained version. A framed print or a plain-coloured mug with a small duck reads as taste. A hat with a duck on every panel reads as a stag do. Both have their place — just know which one you are buying.

What is the best duck gift under £10?

Socks, if they will be worn; a sticker sheet, if the recipient is under about 25; hair clips, if they have hair to clip. All three get used almost immediately.

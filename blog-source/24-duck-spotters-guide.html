People who buy duck things are not always people who look at ducks, and the two overlap less than you would expect. This is for the crossover: what you are actually looking at on a pond, and which duck is which when someone puts one on a mug.

The five you will actually see in the UK

Mallard. The default duck. The male has the bottle-green head, white neck ring and grey body; the female is mottled brown and considerably better camouflaged, which is the point. Both have a blue-purple patch on the wing called a speculum, edged in white, which is the reliable way to identify a female mallard when you cannot see anything else useful.

Tufted duck. Small, black and white, with a little ponytail off the back of the head and a startling yellow eye. Dives rather than dabbles, so it will vanish mid-sentence. Extremely common on urban lakes and reservoirs and frequently mistaken for something exotic.

Mandarin. The absurd one. Orange sail-shaped feathers standing up off the back, a purple chest, a white eye stripe. Introduced from East Asia, escaped from collections, now genuinely established in Britain. If you see one you will know.

Pekin. The big white farm duck with the orange bill and the upright posture. This is the domestic duck, and it is the one that most looks like a rubber duck in real life — which is not a coincidence, since it is the shape the toy was abstracted from.

Indian Runner. Stands nearly vertical and runs rather than waddles. Looks permanently like it is late for something. Kept domestically, occasionally seen semi-wild, and a personal favourite for reasons of posture alone.

Duck, goose or swan

The one to know: the "silly goose" of internet fame is a goose, not a duck, and the aesthetic is deliberately different — geese are drawn menacing, ducks are drawn oblivious. Both appear in the range and they are doing different jobs.

Two things that are widely believed and wrong

"A duck's quack doesn't echo." It does. This was tested properly at the University of Salford in 2003 with a duck named Daisy in an anechoic chamber and then a reverberation chamber. The quack echoes; the echo is just hard to hear, because a quack fades out gradually and the echo blends into the tail of it.

"Only female ducks quack." Nearly true, and worth knowing. The classic loud quack is the female mallard. Males make a quieter, rasping call that sounds nothing like the sound effect. So the duck noise everyone imitates is specifically a female mallard.

Bread

Since it comes up: bread is not toxic to ducks, but it is nutritionally close to worthless, and large amounts of it fouling the water is bad for the pond. The current advice from wildlife organisations is to feed sweetcorn, defrosted frozen peas, oats, or proper duck pellets instead. All are cheap and ducks prefer them.

If you are going to feed them anything, feed them peas. They are absurdly keen on peas.

Which duck ends up on what

A note from the drawing side, since the choice is not arbitrary.

  • The rubber duck silhouette — Pekin-derived — is used for anything small. It reads at 2cm where a mallard does not.
  • Mallards get used on prints, where there is room for the green head and the wing patch to be worth drawing.
  • Geese appear where the design needs an attitude rather than a face.
  • Ducklings are the hardest to draw without it becoming saccharine, which is why there are fewer of them than you might expect.

Common questions

What is the most common duck in the UK?

The mallard, by a wide margin. The green-headed male and the mottled brown female are the same species, which surprises people.

Does a duck's quack echo?

Yes. The myth was tested and disproved in 2003. The echo is simply difficult to distinguish because of how the sound fades.

What is the difference between a duck and a goose?

Geese are larger, longer-necked, graze on land, honk rather than quack, and are markedly more confrontational. Ducks are smaller, shorter-necked and feed mainly on the water.

Should you feed bread to ducks?

Better not to. Sweetcorn, defrosted peas, oats or duck pellets are all better for the birds and for the water.

Why is the rubber duck yellow?

Because the domestic Pekin duck is white with an orange bill, and yellow reads better in moulded rubber and against bathwater. The plain yellow version was fixed in popular culture by Sesame Street in 1970 — more on that in why ducks are suddenly everywhere.

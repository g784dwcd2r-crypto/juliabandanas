A duck party is one of the easier themes to do well, because the duck does the work. Yellow, white, a bit of pond green, and a shape everybody recognises at a glance. The failure mode is buying a lot of plastic that goes in a bin at nine o'clock.

Here is the version that mostly leaves things behind that get used again.

Get the palette right and the rest follows

Three colours, no more.

  • Yellow — the duck. Warm, not neon.
  • White or cream — the background. Do most of the room in this.
  • One accent — pond green, sky blue or soft pink, depending on the mood you want.

Yellow is a strong colour and a room done entirely in it is exhausting after an hour. Use it on the small things — plates, ribbon, a few balloons — and keep the large surfaces pale.

For a child's birthday

The duck pond. A washing-up bowl or paddling pool, water, numbered rubber ducks floating in it, a prize matched to each number. It costs almost nothing, it entertains four-year-olds for far longer than it has any right to, and every child leaves holding a duck. Nothing else on this list performs as well.

Duck, duck, goose. Free. Works from about four upward. Ends in someone crying, but every good party game does.

Pin the bill on the duck. A large duck drawn on paper and taped to a door. Ten minutes to make, and it is the one activity that reliably works for a mixed age range.

Sticker station. A table with sticker sheets and plain paper bags or notebooks, and children decorate their own party bag. This solves the party bag problem entirely — they make it themselves, they take it home, and you have not bought forty small plastic objects.

For an adult's

The trick with an adult duck party is to be deadpan rather than childish. It should look like a nice party that happens to have a duck problem, not a soft play centre.

  • One large print on the wall rather than bunting everywhere. A framed duck print reads as a joke made with confidence; forty paper ducks read as a theme applied nervously.
  • Ducks in the drinks. Small rubber ducks in an ice bucket, or one in the punch bowl. Say nothing about it and let people find it.
  • Mugs as the favour. Rather than party bags, a mug each. Cheaper than most favours per head, and it is the only party favour anybody keeps.
  • Yellow and white food. Lemon anything, custard tarts, cheese, mustard. It is a colour scheme rather than a menu, which is more fun to shop for than it sounds.

The favour problem

Party bags are the part everybody dreads. Three approaches, in order of how much I would recommend them.

ApproachCost per headVerdict
One good small thing (sticker sheet, pin, keyring)£3–5Best. Kept rather than binned
Make-your-own at the party£2–3Doubles as an activity. Very hard to beat
A bag of assorted small plastic£4–6Costs more, lasts less. Avoid

The pattern is the same as gift-giving generally: one thing they will keep beats five they will not. That principle runs through the duck lover gift guide as well.

Cake, briefly

A round yellow cake with an orange fondant bill and two chocolate-button eyes takes about fifteen minutes and looks exactly like a duck. This is by some distance the highest ratio of effect to effort available in themed baking, and it is why every duck party has one.

If you cannot face fondant: a plain cake with two or three rubber ducks stood on top. Wash them first. Everyone will assume you meant it.

Baby showers

Ducks are the default baby shower theme for a good reason — they are gender-neutral, universally legible and cheerful without being sentimental. The same rules apply: pale background, yellow accents, one strong focal decoration rather than many small ones.

The one thing worth adding: rubber ducks used as decoration become bath toys afterwards, which makes them the only decoration at the whole event with a second life.

What to skip

Yellow balloon arches. Expensive, take an hour, deflate by the following afternoon.

Themed paper plates. A pound more than plain yellow ones and they are in the bin either way.

Anything that only works in a photograph. If a decoration has no function once the photo is taken, it is a photograph, not a decoration.

Common questions

What colours suit a duck theme?

Warm yellow, white or cream, and one accent — pond green, sky blue or soft pink. Keep the large surfaces pale and put the yellow in the small details.

What are good duck party games for young children?

A numbered duck pond with matched prizes, duck-duck-goose, and pin the bill on the duck. The duck pond is the one that works best and costs least.

What should go in a duck party bag?

One thing worth keeping — a sticker sheet, a pin, a keyring or a small rubber duck — rather than an assortment of small plastic items.

Do ducks work as a baby shower theme?

Very well. Gender-neutral, instantly readable, and the rubber ducks become bath toys afterwards rather than waste.

A rubber duck on a dashboard is a very silly object that people are extremely serious about. There are duck-swapping communities, cowboy hats sized for a 7cm duck, and a genuine etiquette around leaving them on other people's cars.

This is the practical side of it: what to buy, where to put it, and how to stop it becoming a projectile.

The two kinds of dashboard duck

Squeeze ducks are soft vinyl or rubber, hollow, and usually 6–10cm tall. They are light, they cost little, and they are the ones people give away. If you are joining in with any kind of duck-swapping, these are what you want.

Resin and solid ornaments are heavier, more detailed, and meant to stay put. Better as a gift or a permanent fixture, worse as something to leave on a stranger's bonnet.

Most of the range in Duck Mood is the first kind, with hats, sunglasses, chains and tiny scarves, because that is what people actually collect.

Where it should go

The honest answer: somewhere it cannot hit you.

A loose ornament on a dashboard is fine at 20mph and a genuine hazard under emergency braking. It is worth thirty seconds of thought.

PositionVerdict
Fixed to the dashboard with an adhesive padBest. Stays put, visible from outside, no windscreen glare
Loose on top of the dashboardOnly for very light squeeze ducks, and only with a non-slip mat
Clipped to an air ventGood for small charms. Avoid on the hot-air vent in winter

Nothing on the dashboard should be heavy enough to hurt if it came off. If you would not want it thrown at you, do not put it where an airbag or a hard stop can throw it.

Making it stay put

Three methods, in ascending order of commitment.

  1. A silicone non-slip mat. The dashboard-phone-holder kind. Grips well, leaves no residue, lifts off in a second. Best first choice.
  2. Removable adhesive putty. Cheap, effective, and it goes gummy in summer heat. Fine for a season, less fine for a year.
  3. A double-sided foam pad. Permanent in practice. It will hold through anything and it may mark the dashboard when it eventually comes off. Only use this where you are certain.

Whatever you use, clean the dashboard first with a little rubbing alcohol. Dashboards get sprayed with silicone-based interior polish, and nothing sticks to that — it is designed so nothing does.

The sun problem

A car interior in July gets far hotter than the weather outside — comfortably 50°C on a dashboard behind glass, sometimes more. Two things follow.

Colour fades. Yellow vinyl in direct sun goes pale, then slightly orange, over a summer. There is nothing to be done about it beyond parking in the shade or accepting a weathered duck as character.

Soft vinyl deforms. A hollow squeeze duck left in full sun against the windscreen can slump. Not usually dramatically, but the hat may end up at a jauntier angle than intended.

If you want one to stay pristine, keep it out of the direct strip along the front of the dashboard and use a sunshade in summer.

Cleaning one

Warm water, a drop of washing-up liquid, a soft cloth. Dashboards accumulate a fine, greasy dust that comes off easily and looks awful once you notice it.

Do not use interior wipes containing solvents on printed detail — the eyes and cheeks are the first things to go. And take the tiny hat off before you wash it, unless you enjoy the tiny hat being wet for three days.

The etiquette, briefly

If you are getting into the duck-leaving side of it — placing ducks on other people's cars — there are some settled conventions. They are covered properly in Duck Duck Jeep, explained, but the short version is: on the door handle or wing mirror, never under a wiper, never on a car that is not part of it, and never anywhere that scratches paint.

Common questions

Are dashboard ornaments legal in the UK?

There is no law against an ornament on the dashboard itself. The rule that matters is that your view of the road must not be obstructed, which is why hanging items from the mirror is the risky one. Keep it low, keep it small, keep it out of the swept area of the windscreen.

Will an ornament damage my dashboard?

A non-slip mat will not. Adhesive putty can leave an oily mark on soft-touch plastics if left for a year in the heat. Permanent foam pads can lift the finish when removed. Start with the mat.

Will the colour fade?

Yes, eventually, in a car that lives outside. A summer of direct sun visibly lightens yellow vinyl. Most people find they quite like the faded one by then.

What size duck is best for a dashboard?

Around 6–8cm. Small enough not to block anything, large enough to be seen from outside the car, light enough to be safe.

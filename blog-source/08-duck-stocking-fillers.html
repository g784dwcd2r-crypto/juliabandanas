Secret Santa has a budget, and the budget is the problem. Fifteen pounds is enough to buy something genuinely nice and more than enough to buy something that gets left on the desk on the last day of term.

Here is what actually works at that price, and the reasoning underneath it, so you can apply the same logic anywhere.

The test a cheap gift has to pass

One question: will this be used in the first week?

That is it. A £4 sticker sheet that ends up on a laptop on Boxing Day is a better gift than a £14 ornament that goes in a cupboard. Cheap gifts fail by being decorative; they succeed by being useful, wearable or edible.

The corollary: never buy a cheap version of an expensive thing. A bad £12 hat is worse than a good £6 pair of socks, because the hat invites comparison and the socks do not.

Under £5: the sticker and pin bracket

Sticker sheets are the most reliable filler in the shop. They fit in a card, they weigh nothing to post, and the recipient does something with them the same day. For an office Secret Santa where you barely know the person, this is the safe answer. Stickers and small things.

Enamel pins cost a little more and land a little harder. A pin goes on a coat or a tote and stays there for years. If the person has a visible bag, buy the pin.

Both are covered properly in where to put a sticker and the enamel pin guide if you want to include a bit of useful advice with the gift.

Socks are the workhorse of this bracket for a reason: they are unsized in practice, they get worn within days, and they wear out, which means they never become clutter.

£10 to £15: one proper object

At the top of a Secret Santa budget you can buy something that looks like a real present rather than a filler.

A mug is the best of them. It is the single most-used object on most desks, it survives being given to someone whose taste you do not know, and it does not need sizing. Mugs and cups.

A car ornament is the funniest of them, and better value than it looks. For anyone who drives, a duck on the dashboard gets seen constantly and started an entire waving culture — see Duck Duck Jeep.

A small print is the most grown-up of them. Unframed, it posts flat and cheap; framed by the recipient later, it becomes a proper thing.

Building a £15 bundle instead

Three small things wrapped together beat one £15 item more often than not, because unwrapping three things is simply better than unwrapping one. A workable combination:

  • Socks (worn that week)
  • A sticker sheet (used that day)
  • A pin or keyring (kept for years)

That covers immediate use, daily use and long-term keeping, which is roughly what a good present does at any price.

Things to avoid at this budget

Anything sized. Shirts and slippers are lovely gifts and terrible Secret Santa gifts, because you cannot ask for a size without ruining the surprise.

Anything breakable that has to travel in a rucksack. Mugs are fine posted; mugs are less fine carried home on a night out.

Anything with an in-joke you are not certain about. The sarcastic designs are excellent for close friends and a small disaster for the colleague you drew out of a hat.

Common questions

What is a good Secret Santa gift under £10?

Socks, a sticker sheet, a pin or a keyring. All four are used within a week and none of them require knowing anything about the recipient.

What should I put in a stocking for someone who likes ducks?

One wearable thing (socks), one sticky thing (stickers), one keepable thing (a pin or charm), and a mug if the stocking is large enough to take it.

Do stickers make a good gift on their own?

For anyone under about 25, yes — a sheet is a proper small present. For older recipients, pair it with something else, because a single sticker sheet reads as an afterthought even when it is not.

How early should I order for Christmas?

Late November at the latest. Stock is not replenished mid-season, so the constraint is availability rather than postage.

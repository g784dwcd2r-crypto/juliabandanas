Children's sizing is labelled by age, which is the least useful measurement available, because children of the same age vary by about 20cm. Everyone knows this and everyone still buys by age.

Here is how to do it properly in about two minutes, plus the safety bits that matter and the ones that do not.

Height, not age

Nearly all children's clothing charts convert age to height internally. Skip the middle step and measure the child.

Stand them against a doorframe without shoes, mark the top of the head with a pencil, measure. Write the number and the date on the frame like everybody's grandparents did, and you will never have to do it again from scratch.

Size up, but only by one

A child grows roughly 5–7cm a year between four and ten. One size up buys you most of a year of wear; two sizes up buys you a garment that looks wrong for six months and is then briefly right.

The exception is anything worn against the skin for sport or sleep, where a good fit matters more than longevity.

Where children's sizing goes wrong

Imported sizing runs small. Many printed kids' tees are made to Asian sizing and run one to two years smaller than the UK label suggests. A "6–7 years" from that chart often fits a UK five-year-old. If a product lists centimetres, trust those over the age.

Body length varies more than chest. Kids' tops are the right width and the wrong length far more often than the reverse. Check the length figure on the size chart if there is one.

Neck openings. The single most common complaint from parents of toddlers. A t-shirt that fits everywhere else and has a tight neck is a t-shirt that produces a fight every morning. Look for an envelope neck (the overlapping shoulder opening) on anything under about age three.

Fabric for children specifically

Cotton, and preferably 150gsm or above. Two reasons beyond comfort: thin fabric shows every mark, and thin fabric goes through at the elbow within a term.

A small amount of elastane (around 5%) in a kids' tee is a genuine improvement — it recovers its shape after being pulled, which children do constantly.

Avoid pure polyester next to the skin for young children. It does not breathe, and children run hot.

The safety points that actually matter

Not scaremongering — these are the specific things UK and EU children's clothing standards exist to cover, and they are worth knowing when buying from anywhere.

  • No cords or drawstrings at the neck or hood on clothing for children under seven. This is the big one and it is regulated for good reason.
  • Small attachments should be firmly fixed. Buttons, badges and appliqués on clothing for under-threes are a choking risk if they can be pulled off. Give anything new a firm tug before it goes on.
  • Nightwear has its own flammability rules. A cotton t-shirt is not nightwear, and should not be bought as a substitute for it for a small child.
  • Wash before first wear. Removes finishing chemicals and any dye that was going to come off anyway.

Prints and children

Two practical notes. First, a large print across the chest of a small garment is stiffer than the fabric around it, and on a very young child that can feel odd — smaller prints sit better on smaller sizes.

Second, children choose their own clothes earlier than parents expect. From about four, a child who picked the duck shirt will wear the duck shirt to destruction, and a child who did not will not wear it at all. If they are old enough to have an opinion, get the opinion.

Washing so it survives a school year

Inside out, 30°C, no fabric softener, no high-heat tumble drying. Same as any printed cotton — the full reasoning is in washing printed cotton.

For the specific horrors of children's clothing: grass stains respond to washing-up liquid worked in before washing; anything greasy responds to the same; and everything responds better if you get to it before it dries.

Common questions

How do I choose a kids' t-shirt size?

Measure the child's height without shoes and match it to the height range on the chart. Age labels are a rough proxy for height and nothing more.

Should I buy a bigger size for a child to grow into?

One size up is sensible and gives most of a year of extra wear. Two sizes up means a garment that fits badly for most of the time you own it.

Do kids' clothes with prints shrink?

Cotton shrinks in length in the first hot wash. Wash at 30°C and air dry and it stays put.

What should I check before a small child wears something new?

No cords at the neck, no attachments that can be pulled off, and a wash before first wear. Then check the neck opening will actually go over their head without a struggle.

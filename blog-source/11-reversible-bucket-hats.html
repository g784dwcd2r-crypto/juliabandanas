A reversible bucket hat is two hats, and most people only ever wear one of them. It is worth knowing what you have got, because the second side is usually the more useful one.

How they are built

A reversible hat is two complete hats sewn together at the brim edge and turned through. There is no lining and no right side — both faces are finished, both are meant to be seen, and you flip it inside out to change which one is.

Two consequences follow, and they explain nearly everything about how these hats behave.

The band is thicker. Two layers of cotton sit against your head instead of one, so a reversible hat fits fractionally tighter than a single-layer hat of the same stated size. If your head measures at the very top of a size range — 58cm on a 56–58cm hat — expect it to be snug at first. It gives after a week.

It takes longer to dry. Two layers, no ventilation between them. Not a problem in normal use; a real problem if you tumble-dry it, which you should not.

Choosing which side is which

Most reversible hats pair a loud side with a quiet one — an all-over print against a plain colour, or a big graphic against a small repeated one. That is the useful combination, and it is worth buying deliberately.

SituationSide to wear
Festival, beach, holidayThe loud one, obviously
Walking the dog on a TuesdayPlain
Meeting someone's parentsPlain

Sun protection and nothing elseWhichever is lighter in colour

That last row is not a joke. A pale crown reflects noticeably more heat than a black one on a hot day, and if you have the choice built into the hat, use it.

How to actually flip one

Push the crown through from the inside, then work around the brim with your fingers to bring the seam fully out. Do not pull on the brim edge — that is the seam holding both hats together, and it is the one part of the hat with a job.

Once flipped, put it on a fist or a ball and smooth the crown back into shape. Reversible hats crease at the crown more than single-layer ones because there is more fabric to fold.

Fit, in one paragraph

Measure around your head just above the ears, keep the tape level, snug but not tight, and read the centimetres rather than the letter. Most bucket hats here are 56–58cm and that genuinely does fit a wide range of adults. If you are between sizes on a reversible hat, go down rather than up — the extra layer takes up the slack. Washing without ruining either side

The brim is the vulnerable part. Most bucket hat brims are stiffened with an interfacing that does not enjoy heat, and a hot wash can leave it wavy in a way that never fully recovers.

  • Hand wash in cool water with a little non-biological detergent. Ten minutes, gentle squeezing, no wringing.
  • If you must machine wash: 30°C, delicate cycle, inside a pillowcase or wash bag.
  • Never tumble dry. The heat is what warps brims and shrinks the band.
  • Reshape while wet over a bowl, a ball or a rolled towel, and dry away from direct sun.
  • No fabric softener. It sits on printed cotton and dulls the colour.

Both faces of a reversible hat are printed, so both are at risk. There is more on why heat and softener are the two things that kill printed cotton in washing printed cotton.

Packing

Bucket hats pack better than any other hat, which is most of their appeal. Roll it rather than folding it flat, stuff a pair of socks inside the crown, and it will come out of a rucksack in the shape it went in. A folded brim will hold a crease for a day or two; a rolled one will not.

Common questions

Do reversible bucket hats fit smaller than normal ones?

Slightly, yes. Two layers of fabric in the band take up a few millimetres. If you are at the top of a size range, expect snug for the first week.

Can you wear a reversible bucket hat in the rain?

Yes. Cotton will get wet and will dry, just slowly, because there are two layers. Reshape it while damp rather than leaving it flat on a radiator.

How do you get a bucket hat back into shape?

Dampen the crown slightly, put it over something round — a ball, an upturned bowl, a rolled towel — and let it dry at room temperature. Never on a radiator and never in a tumble dryer.

Which side of a reversible hat is the "right" side?

Neither. Both are finished and both are intended to be worn. If there is a label, it usually sits in the crown of the side the maker photographed, which is a hint rather than a rule.

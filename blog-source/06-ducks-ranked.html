Season 01 contains a lot of ducks. Roughly forty pieces, and every one of them has an opinion of itself.

What follows is a ranking of the recurring duck archetypes by how seriously they appear to take their own existence. This is not a buying guide. It has, however, been consulted as one.

1. The rubber duck, unmodified

Takes itself least seriously and is therefore the most successful. A plain yellow rubber duck has no ambitions beyond being a rubber duck. It reads instantly at any size, it works on a hat and a print and a sock without adjustment, and nobody has ever had to explain it to anybody.

It is the load-bearing duck of the entire season.

2. The duck in a bucket hat

A duck wearing the exact item you are buying. This should be too clever by half and somehow is not, probably because the duck looks so pleased about it.

Consistently one of the fastest-selling things in the season, which suggests the joke lands on other people too.

3. The cowboy duck

Hat, sometimes a scarf, occasionally sunglasses. Takes itself extremely seriously, which is why it works — the humour is entirely in the duck's own commitment to the bit.

Appears most often as a dashboard ornament, where it stares straight ahead down the road with total conviction.

4. The wine duck

A duck with a glass of wine, usually in a colour palette borrowed from a 1970s dinner party. This is the one that survives being framed, and it is the one most often bought for a kitchen.

It takes itself moderately seriously, in the manner of someone who has had one glass and is enjoying the evening.

5. Ducks in a row

Several ducks, one line, usually accompanied by a caption about not having them in one. The most common duck joke there is, and the ranking suffers for it.

Redeemed entirely by the compositions where the ducks are visibly not in a row, which is a better joke than the caption.

6. The sleeping duck

Eyes closed, sitting down, no notes. The quiet corner of the season and the one that ends up in the largest number of bedrooms.

Takes itself seriously in the way that anything asleep does, which is to say not at all, but with dignity.

7. The duck with a slogan

Ranges from genuinely funny to the sort of thing printed on a mug in a service station. The good ones are very good. The bad ones are why this category sits at seven.

The test is whether you would still find it funny in eighteen months. Most fail it.

8. The goose, wrongly filed

Every season has an intruder. Several pieces in Season 01 are not ducks at all — they are geese, and specifically the silly-goose genre of goose, which has its own established personality and considerable self-importance.

They are here because they are good, and because nobody standing in a hallway looking at a poster has ever once said "that is technically an anseriform of a different genus".

9. The Christmas duck

Santa hat, string of lights, full commitment. Takes itself the most seriously of any duck in the season and is, for eleven months of the year, completely unwearable.

Bottom of the ranking and top of the sales chart every December, which is its own kind of answer.

What this actually tells you

The pattern, if there is one, is that the ducks doing the least work land the best. The plain rubber duck outsells the elaborate ones. The sleeping duck outlasts the shouting one. The pieces that are still good in a year are almost always the quiet ones.

Which is a slightly unexpected thing to learn from a season about ducks, and probably applies more widely than it should.
